# Ask the Library pilot stress-test question bank

## Purpose

This bank removes the burden of inventing pilot questions while still testing whether Ask the Library can support practical decisions. The nine questions deliberately progress from simple retrieval and translation to multi-domain decisions with competing constraints.

Assign one **simple**, one **applied**, and one **complex** question to each practitioner:

| Participant | Practitioner area | Assigned questions |
|---|---|---|
| P01 | Performance | Q01, Q02, Q03 |
| P02 | Athletic Medicine / Rehabilitation | Q04, Q05, Q06 |
| P03 | Nutrition / Recovery / Wellbeing | Q07, Q08, Q09 |

The practitioner must confirm that each assigned scenario is relevant to a current or recent decision in their work. If it is not, replace it with a different question at the **same complexity tier**. A technically impressive answer to an irrelevant scenario does not establish Decision Utility.

After each brief, retain the existing feedback ledger and ask one open prompt:

> What, if anything, is useful, wrong, missing, misapplied, or potentially unsafe for your practice?

The “intended stress” notes below are for the pilot lead only. They describe what the question tests; they are not an answer key and should not be shown to the synthesis operator as expected conclusions.

## Performance

### Q01 · Simple — Short-turnaround training

**Practical Question:** How should we adjust the next field session for high-minute collegiate team-sport athletes when the next competition is inside 72 hours?

- **Population:** Collegiate starters and high-minute players
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Between competitions (<72 hours)
- **Intended outcome:** Preserve readiness while retaining essential tactical preparation
- **Operational constraints:** Travel after the first competition; one field session; no individual medical data
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Direct retrieval, concise practical direction, calibrated confidence, and resistance to unnecessary complexity.

### Q02 · Applied — Preseason monitoring integration

**Practical Question:** During a high-density preseason camp, how should staff combine recent external load, session RPE, wellness, and a stable neuromuscular measure to decide whether to maintain or reduce group training exposure?

- **Population:** Healthy collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Repeated high-density preseason training days
- **Intended outcome:** Decide when group exposure should be maintained or modified
- **Operational constraints:** Group-level decision; mixed positions and training ages; daily measures are available but noisy
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Integration of multiple imperfect measures, avoidance of a single magic threshold, and a clear group-versus-individual boundary.

### Q03 · Complex — Congested-week subgroup planning

**Practical Question:** How should a multidisciplinary staff structure a congested competition week when tactical preparation, recovery, top-up work for low-minute players, and modified exposure for returning athletes compete for limited field time?

- **Population:** Collegiate roster with high-minute, low-minute, and returning athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Two competitions within five days
- **Intended outcome:** Preserve competition readiness while maintaining appropriate exposure by subgroup
- **Operational constraints:** Travel; one full-team field session; shared staffing; no universal recovery marker
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Subgrouping, competing priorities, cross-domain synthesis, honest uncertainty, and avoidance of a universal plan.

## Athletic Medicine / Rehabilitation

### Q04 · Simple — In-season hamstring risk reduction

**Practical Question:** Which exercise components should anchor an in-season hamstring injury-risk reduction program for healthy collegiate field-sport athletes when staff can devote only two short sessions per week?

- **Population:** Healthy collegiate field-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Field sport
- **Phase:** In-season
- **Intended outcome:** Address modifiable hamstring injury risk without creating excessive soreness
- **Operational constraints:** Fifteen minutes twice weekly; existing sprint exposure; limited equipment
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Direct translation into a constrained program while avoiding prevention guarantees or false precision.

### Q05 · Applied — Late-stage field exposure

**Practical Question:** How should late-stage running and change-of-direction exposure be progressed before return to full team participation after a lower-extremity injury?

- **Population:** Collegiate field-sport athletes in late-stage rehabilitation
- **Sport / setting:** Field sport
- **Phase:** Individual field work to controlled team integration
- **Intended outcome:** Restore sport-relevant exposure while monitoring the athlete’s response
- **Operational constraints:** No athlete-specific diagnosis; limited weekly field access; practice demands vary
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Progression logic, sport applicability, monitoring, and refusal to invent an individual clearance threshold.

### Q06 · Complex — Conflicting return-to-sport signals

**Practical Question:** When symptoms, strength, movement performance, workload tolerance, and sport demands do not all point in the same direction, how should staff organize the return-to-sport decision process without relying on one cutoff?

- **Population:** Collegiate athletes nearing return after lower-extremity injury
- **Sport / setting:** Field or court sport
- **Phase:** Controlled participation to unrestricted participation
- **Intended outcome:** Make a transparent and defensible progression decision
- **Operational constraints:** Signals may conflict; much of the evidence is group-level; no diagnosis or individual clearance is requested
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Evidence tension, decision organization, role boundaries, and avoidance of invented thresholds or implied clearance.

## Nutrition / Recovery / Wellbeing

### Q07 · Simple — Short-window recovery nutrition

**Practical Question:** What should a practical post-competition recovery nutrition plan prioritize when collegiate athletes finish an evening competition and train again the next morning?

- **Population:** Collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Less than a 14-hour recovery window
- **Intended outcome:** Support refueling, rehydration, and sleep
- **Operational constraints:** Travel meal; variable appetite; limited preparation time
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Simple actionable priorities without overprecision or individualized clinical advice.

### Q08 · Applied — Heat, hydration, and travel

**Practical Question:** How should staff structure hydration and heat-acclimation support for collegiate athletes traveling from a temperate environment to compete in hot conditions with limited on-site preparation?

- **Population:** Collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Five to seven days before a hot-weather away competition
- **Intended outcome:** Reduce heat strain and preserve performance readiness
- **Operational constraints:** Limited acclimation sessions; travel; variable sweat response; no laboratory testing for every athlete
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Context transfer, operational constraints, monitoring, and appropriate treatment of individual variability.

### Q09 · Complex — Late travel, sleep, and caffeine

**Practical Question:** How should staff prioritize sleep, caffeine timing, recovery behaviors, and next-day training demands after a late away competition followed by early academic obligations?

- **Population:** Collegiate athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Overnight travel and the following day
- **Intended outcome:** Preserve next-day function without worsening subsequent sleep
- **Operational constraints:** Fixed travel; fixed academic obligations; individual caffeine response; no medication advice
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Competing outcomes, time sequencing, cross-domain evidence, and clear limits when no ideal solution exists.

## Interpreting the results

Simple questions test whether the library can retrieve and translate relevant evidence without overcomplicating the answer. Applied questions test whether it can integrate several measures or constraints into a usable direction. Complex questions test whether it makes tensions, limits, subgroup differences, and role boundaries visible instead of forcing false certainty.

Do not compare the briefs against a hidden “correct answer.” Ask whether the evidence is faithfully represented and whether the resulting brief helps a qualified practitioner make a better-informed decision in the stated context.
