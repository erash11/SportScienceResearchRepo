# Ask the Library pilot stress-test question bank

## Purpose

This bank contains 12 copy-ready question-and-context sets: one simple, one applied, and one complex question for each of four practitioner domains. The progression tests whether Ask the Library can move from focused evidence translation to decisions involving multiple signals, constraints, and professional boundaries.

The bank is a prompt source, not a prototype upload. Copy the **Practical Question** and all five **Decision Context** fields into the main page, then select **Save question for Codex**. Do not choose the question-bank JSON as a finished answer file.

## Selecting tracks for the current pilot

The minimum credible pilot remains three participants and nine questions:

1. Match each participant to the domain track that best reflects their role.
2. Select three of the four tracks for the three-person pilot.
3. Give each participant all three questions in the selected track: simple, applied, and complex.
4. Keep the fourth track as an alternate. Do not count all 12 questions in the current scorecard unless the pilot contract is intentionally revised.

| Domain track | Simple | Applied | Complex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical Therapy | PT01 | PT02 | PT03 |
| Performance Nutrition | PN01 | PN02 | PN03 |
| Sports Science | SS01 | SS02 | SS03 |
| Sports Performance | SP01 | SP02 | SP03 |

The practitioner must confirm that each assigned scenario is relevant to a current or recent decision in their work. If it is not, replace it with a different question at the **same complexity tier and within the same domain**. A technically impressive answer to an irrelevant scenario does not establish Decision Utility.

After each brief, retain the existing feedback ledger and ask one open prompt:

> What, if anything, is useful, wrong, missing, misapplied, or potentially unsafe for your practice?

The “intended stress” notes below are for the pilot lead only. They describe what the question tests; they are not an answer key and should not be shown to the synthesis operator as expected conclusions.

## Physical Therapy

### PT01 · Simple — Progressing rehabilitation intensity

**Practical Question:** Which criteria should a physical therapist prioritize when deciding whether a collegiate field- or court-sport athlete is ready to progress from controlled rehabilitation drills to a higher-intensity field or court session?

- **Population:** Collegiate athlete in late-stage lower-extremity rehabilitation
- **Sport / setting:** Field or court sport
- **Phase:** Planned progression from controlled drills to higher-intensity individual work
- **Intended outcome:** Make a defensible next-step progression decision while monitoring response
- **Operational constraints:** De-identified scenario; no diagnosis or clearance request; available evidence is primarily group-level
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Focused criteria retrieval, calibrated confidence, and refusal to turn one test or threshold into clearance.

### PT02 · Applied — Entry to modified team practice

**Practical Question:** How should symptoms, strength, movement quality, and sport-specific running or cutting exposure be combined when deciding whether to advance a collegiate athlete from individual rehabilitation to modified team practice?

- **Population:** Collegiate athlete returning after a lower-extremity injury
- **Sport / setting:** Field or court sport
- **Phase:** Individual rehabilitation to modified team participation
- **Intended outcome:** Organize a transparent progress-or-hold decision using several relevant signals
- **Operational constraints:** Signals may be collected on different days; team demands vary; medical decision authority remains with the care team
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Integration of clinical and performance information without inventing weights, cutoffs, or an implied clearance decision.

### PT03 · Complex — Normal clinic measures, incomplete sport exposure

**Practical Question:** When symptoms and clinic-based measures have normalized but high-speed and sport-specific exposure remain incomplete, how should physical therapy and performance staff structure the next 7–10 days and define progress, hold, or regress criteria without relying on a single return-to-sport cutoff?

- **Population:** Collegiate athlete nearing unrestricted participation after a lower-extremity injury
- **Sport / setting:** Field sport with sprinting, cutting, and reactive demands
- **Phase:** Modified participation to unrestricted team activity
- **Intended outcome:** Coordinate staged exposure and decision ownership across rehabilitation and performance staff
- **Operational constraints:** Limited practice opportunities; competition schedule continues; no athlete-identifying or diagnosis-specific data
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Evidence tension, time sequencing, shared-role boundaries, and an honest decision framework when clinical and sport-exposure signals disagree.

## Performance Nutrition

### PN01 · Simple — Early-morning pre-training fuel

**Practical Question:** What should pre-training fueling and hydration prioritize for a collegiate athlete completing an early-morning high-intensity session when appetite is low and less than 45 minutes are available before training?

- **Population:** Collegiate athletes participating in high-intensity morning training
- **Sport / setting:** Team-sport training environment
- **Phase:** Before an early-morning field or court session
- **Intended outcome:** Support session quality with a practical, tolerable intake approach
- **Operational constraints:** Low appetite; limited preparation time; individual gastrointestinal tolerance; no clinical nutrition condition
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Direct evidence translation into priorities without false precision, individualized medical advice, or a universal meal prescription.

### PN02 · Applied — Fueling a two-session day

**Practical Question:** How should carbohydrate, fluid, sodium, and recovery intake be organized across a same-day lift and field-practice schedule when the goal is to support both sessions without creating unnecessary gastrointestinal burden?

- **Population:** Collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Strength session followed later by field practice
- **Phase:** High-demand two-session training day
- **Intended outcome:** Preserve training quality and between-session recovery
- **Operational constraints:** Four to six hours between sessions; campus dining options; variable sweat loss and gastrointestinal tolerance
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Sequencing and individualization across multiple nutrition targets without inventing unsupported gram, fluid, or sodium prescriptions.

### PN03 · Complex — Congested travel-week nutrition

**Practical Question:** During a seven-day travel period with two competitions, limited food access, and athletes with different playing-time demands, how should performance nutrition prioritize and individualize fueling, hydration, recovery, and supplement decisions while protecting sleep and gastrointestinal tolerance?

- **Population:** Collegiate roster with high-minute, low-minute, and returning athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Traveling team sport
- **Phase:** Two competitions within seven days
- **Intended outcome:** Create an operational nutrition plan that supports readiness across subgroups
- **Operational constraints:** Fixed travel and hotel meals; limited refrigeration; variable minutes and sweat response; supplement and clinical decisions require appropriate review
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Subgrouping, competing priorities, supplement-governance boundaries, and honest uncertainty when an ideal individualized plan is not feasible.

## Sports Science

### SS01 · Simple — Is a monitoring change real?

**Practical Question:** How should a sports scientist decide whether a change in an athlete’s countermovement-jump measure is large enough to treat as meaningful rather than normal measurement noise?

- **Population:** Healthy collegiate athletes monitored repeatedly
- **Sport / setting:** Team-sport performance program using the same force-platform system
- **Phase:** Weekly in-season monitoring
- **Intended outcome:** Distinguish a potentially meaningful within-athlete change from expected test variability
- **Operational constraints:** Same protocol and metric; limited repeated trials; no diagnosis or readiness clearance is requested
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Reliability and measurement-error translation without presenting one universal threshold as a decision rule.

### SS02 · Applied — Conflicting load and readiness signals

**Practical Question:** How should sports science staff interpret a week in which external load is elevated, athlete-reported wellness worsens, but a standardized neuromuscular test remains stable?

- **Population:** Collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** In-season training and competition monitoring
- **Phase:** Review of the current seven-day microcycle
- **Intended outcome:** Decide what follow-up is warranted before changing group or individual training exposure
- **Operational constraints:** Measures have different error and timing; individual baselines vary; no single measure is designated as the gold standard
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Triangulation, timing, and signal-specific limitations without averaging unlike measures into a false composite.

### SS03 · Complex — Multiple systems disagree

**Practical Question:** When workload, wellness, neuromuscular, and schedule-based indicators disagree during a congested week, how should the sports science process account for measurement error, individual baselines, and the cost of false alarms before recommending whether training should be maintained, modified, or investigated further?

- **Population:** Collegiate roster with different positions, minutes, and training histories
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport using multiple monitoring systems
- **Phase:** Congested competition week
- **Intended outcome:** Produce a transparent decision-support process rather than a single readiness score
- **Operational constraints:** Missing data; multiple comparisons; limited time for retesting; staff must distinguish group planning from individual follow-up
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Conflicting evidence, false-positive control, individual-versus-group interpretation, and explicit decision thresholds grounded in local reliability rather than invented cutoffs.

## Sports Performance

### SP01 · Simple — Low-volume pre-competition power

**Practical Question:** What training qualities and dose constraints should guide a low-volume strength-and-power session 48 hours before competition when the goal is to preserve readiness without creating unnecessary fatigue?

- **Population:** Healthy collegiate team-sport athletes
- **Sport / setting:** Strength-and-conditioning environment
- **Phase:** Forty-eight hours before competition
- **Intended outcome:** Retain a meaningful performance stimulus while limiting residual fatigue
- **Operational constraints:** Thirty-minute session; existing sport practice; no athlete-specific medical restrictions
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Concise programming principles without inventing a universal set-and-repetition prescription.

### SP02 · Applied — Short-turnaround subgroup training

**Practical Question:** How should high-minute, low-minute, and returning athletes receive different strength, speed, and conditioning exposures during a short turnaround between competitions?

- **Population:** Collegiate roster with different recent competition exposures
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport
- **Phase:** Seventy-two to ninety-six hours between competitions
- **Intended outcome:** Preserve readiness while addressing underexposure and continued progression by subgroup
- **Operational constraints:** One full-team field session; limited weight-room access; returning athletes remain under medical and rehabilitation plans
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Subgrouping and coordination across staff without prescribing a universal top-up dose or overriding return-to-sport authority.

### SP03 · Complex — Seven-day mixed-availability plan

**Practical Question:** Across a seven-day period containing competition, travel, limited field time, and mixed athlete availability, how should sports performance staff allocate strength, speed, conditioning, and recovery exposures while defining individual progress, hold, or adjust criteria?

- **Population:** Collegiate roster with starters, non-starters, and athletes in modified participation
- **Sport / setting:** Team sport with shared field and weight-room resources
- **Phase:** Competition-to-competition microcycle
- **Intended outcome:** Build a coherent plan that preserves competition readiness and appropriate chronic exposure
- **Operational constraints:** Travel; one full-team field session; varied recent minutes; no universal readiness marker; medical plans remain controlling for restricted athletes
- **Pilot lead only — intended stress:** Time sequencing, competing adaptations, subgroup logic, monitoring, and professional boundaries under real operational constraints.

## Interpreting the results

Simple questions test whether the library can retrieve and translate relevant evidence without overcomplicating the answer. Applied questions test whether it can integrate several measures or constraints into a usable direction. Complex questions test whether it makes tensions, limits, subgroup differences, measurement uncertainty, and professional boundaries visible instead of forcing false certainty.

Do not compare the briefs against a hidden “correct answer.” Ask whether the evidence is faithfully represented and whether the resulting brief helps a qualified practitioner make a better-informed decision in the stated context.
