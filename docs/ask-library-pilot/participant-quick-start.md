# Ask the Library Pilot

## Participant quick start

Ask the Library turns one practical health or performance question into a short, source-grounded Decision Brief. You use the website; the pilot lead handles the files and Codex.

### Your role in the 14-day pilot

- Bring **three real questions** that could affect your day-to-day work.
- Read each Decision Brief; the Operational View is designed to take about two minutes.
- Complete about 60–90 seconds of feedback after each brief.

### Before each session

**Choose a real decision.** Bring something you are currently planning, discussing, monitoring, or deciding—not a general topic you are merely curious about.

**Remove identifying details.** Do not include athlete names, initials, roster numbers, dates of birth, medical records, clinical notes, contact details, or any other information that could identify an athlete.

**Bring only the context that could change the answer.** Be ready to describe the population, sport or setting, current phase or timing, intended outcome, and important operational constraints.

### A useful question format

> For **[population]** in **[phase or setting]**, how should we approach **[decision]** to support **[outcome]**, given **[constraints]**?

**Example:** How should we adjust field exposure for collegiate starters between competitions inside 96 hours when travel limits recovery time?

### What happens during each session

1. **Ask.** Enter or paste one de-identified question, add the context that matters, and select **Save question for Codex**. The pilot lead handles the downloaded question file.
2. **Read.** The pilot lead asks Codex to prepare and check the answer, then opens the finished brief for you. Start with the Bottom Line, Recommended Direction or Decision Boundary, Evidence Confidence, and Guardrails.
3. **Rate.** Complete the short feedback form: Did it help? Did it affect the decision? What was missing? How long did it take to understand?

That is your whole role: **ask, read, and rate**. You do not need to use Codex, manage JSON files, open GitHub, or run commands.

### How to interpret the brief

- **On-Demand / Not Expert-Reviewed** means the brief was prepared for this pilot and has not been promoted to a formally reviewed Baylor resource.
- **Evidence Confidence** explains how much confidence the available evidence supports for this specific context.
- **Coverage Gap** is a valid result. It means the library does not currently support a trustworthy direction.
- The brief supports staff judgment. It is not a diagnosis, clearance decision, prescription, or Baylor policy.

### If identifying information is entered

Stop the session and remove it. The question must be resubmitted using group-level or otherwise de-identified context before the pilot can continue.
