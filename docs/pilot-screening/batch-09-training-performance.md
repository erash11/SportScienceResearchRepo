## Decision Notes

1. **Social media and sleep:** Include as a cross-sectional collegiate wellbeing signal. The small Facebook-performance association is not a causal platform effect or a basis for a universal screen-time cutoff.
2. **ACLR strength testing:** Group-level isometric/isokinetic agreement coexists with wide individual limits of agreement. Testing mode should remain consistent within an athlete.
3. **Sled loading:** Fixed percentages of body mass produced different velocity and kinematic responses in sprinters and rugby players. Velocity decrement and mechanics should guide prescription.
4. **Load and injury modeling:** Position- and athlete-specific feature importance supports individualized monitoring, not general injury thresholds.
5. **GPS acceleration-speed profiles:** The automated method is promising for ecological monitoring, but modeled intercepts and measurement error must remain visible and outcome validation is still required.
6. **Flywheel hamstring exercises:** Acute regional MRI responses can inform exercise emphasis; they do not prove longitudinal injury prevention or reinjury reduction.
7. **Compression garments:** Small blood-flow effects are mechanistic surrogates, not confirmation of better recovery or performance. Garment pressure, blinding, and manufacturer-funding context matter.
8. **Resisted sprinting:** Evidence supports acceleration development comparable with unresisted sprinting, with no detected maximum-velocity improvement. Resisted work should complement free sprint exposure.
9. **Warm-up and re-warm-up:** Short progressive preparation, heat retention, and brief explosive reactivation are useful templates, but protocol heterogeneity prevents one universal formula.
10. **Resistance-training velocity:** Fast and moderate-slow repetitions produced similar strength gains overall; the moderate-load advantage for faster work was a nonsignificant trend.
11. **Acute sleep deprivation:** Pooled performance impairment is credible, but sparse and heterogeneous subgroups do not justify precise time-of-day guarantees.
12. **Bilateral versus unilateral training:** Both improved horizontal movement performance without a clear winner, supporting a mixed program chosen around athlete and task constraints.
