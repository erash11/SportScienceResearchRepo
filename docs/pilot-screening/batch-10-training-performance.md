## Decision Notes

1. **Substance-use interference measure:** Include as a brief psychometric conversation aid. It is not a diagnosis, objective performance test, or disciplinary instrument.
2. **College-football player value:** Include as athlete-welfare and institutional context. Economic estimates should not be treated as health evidence or an individual valuation.
3. **Soccer re-warm-up:** A one-minute high-intensity protocol is operationally promising, but the narrative evidence does not demonstrate injury prevention or establish a universal dose.
4. **Sprint EMG asymmetry:** Small-sample bilateral activation patterns are descriptive and cannot establish injury risk or the effect of corrective training.
5. **Instantaneous PlayerLoad:** Movement-strategy timing added information beyond hop distance, but a single-club cross-sectional marker is not a readiness or injury threshold.
6. **NFL Combine:** Position-specific differences provide descriptive norms; combine tests and award outcomes do not form a validated individual success model.
7. **Chronic static stretching:** The pooled jump effect was trivial and sprint effect nonsignificant. Stretching should serve mobility goals rather than replace specific performance training.
8. **Uphill-downhill sprinting:** The small advantage over traditional sprinting came from only six intervention groups with no early-acceleration outcomes.
9. **Post-exercise heat:** Acute findings were mixed and longer-term benefits were mode- and environment-specific. Heat should be treated as added physiological load.
10. **Resisted-sprint load:** Moderate and very heavy loads showed selected advantages, mostly in early acceleration, but bias and sparse female data prevent a universal threshold prescription.
11. **Sleep deprivation:** The updated meta-analysis supports broad performance impairment and higher perceived exertion; heterogeneous subgroups do not supply precise time-of-day rules.
12. **Achilles eccentric training:** Heavy eccentric work outperformed passive or traditional-physiotherapy comparisons in very low-certainty evidence but was not superior to other progressive exercise.
