## Decision Notes

1. **American-football ACLR:** Return and performance outcomes were less favorable than controls in heterogeneous studies; pooled return figures are expectations, not individual clearance rules.
2. **Far-infrared therapy:** One small simulated-match trial in elite women found better recovery markers than sham; it does not establish a required postmatch intervention.
3. **Soccer on-field ACL rehabilitation:** The series supports documenting a graded gap from field rehabilitation to team and match demand, not GPS-only clearance.
4. **NFL physician practice:** The survey describes current surgeon preferences and timing, not comparative outcome evidence.
5. **Lisfranc fixation:** Most of the small series returned, but residual sport pain was common and no comparator was studied.
6. **Disk replacement:** Published athlete cases were sparse, particularly for contact sport; successful reports do not prove broad safety.
7. **Groin pain:** Low-certainty evidence supports individualized active and multimodal rehabilitation, not one universal protocol.
8. **Cartilage restoration queue mismatch:** Excluded because the actual PDF is an unrelated fixture-congestion study.
9. **Lower-extremity testing:** The literature is knee/ACLR-heavy and offers no uniform return criterion.
10. **Team-physician consensus:** Supports an accountable, context-sensitive shared decision process rather than automatic clearance.
11. **Topical glyceryl trinitrate:** Possible adjunct evidence is heterogeneous and must be balanced against adverse effects and progressive loading.
12. **Elite meniscus tears:** Treatment and return decisions require individual tear, knee, sport, and long-term-joint context.
